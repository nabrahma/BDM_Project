0Introduction and the one idea

Home page · 1:15
ShowThe Home page: the title, the five KPI cards and the six page cards.

Good morning, sir. Our project is IPL Auction Value Intelligence. Every year IPL franchises spend hundreds of crores at the auction. Our question is simple: which auction buys actually earned their price?

We took every auction buy from 2013 to 2026, 1,380 buys worth ₹3,269 Cr, and matched them to ball-by-ball data from 921 matches. We matched 99% of the money.

The whole dashboard rests on one idea. First we give every player Impact Points, one score for batting, bowling and fielding: a run is 1 point, a wicket 25, a catch 8. Then the Value Index asks: what share of the league's impact did this player give, compared with the share of the league's money they cost? 1.0 is fair value. Above 1 is a bargain; below 1 means overpaid.

The six cards are our six business questions, and each one is a page. Let's go through them.

1Auction Market

Q5 · 1:00
ShowThe average-price chart (dark bars are mega auctions), then the spend-by-role chart.

This page shows how expensive the auction has become. In 2013 the average buy cost ₹1.7 Cr. At the 2025 mega auction it was ₹3.5 Cr, so prices have doubled.

The dark bars are mega auctions, where teams rebuild their squads; they are always the expensive years. And in 2025–26, bids ended on average 245% above the player's base price.

So what: a franchise should budget about twice the 2013 price for the same player today.

2Franchise Efficiency

Q1 and Q3 · 1:15
ShowThe Value Index bar chart by team (the dashed line is 1.0), then the spend-vs-win scatter.

Which team gets the most for its money? In 2026, Rajasthan Royals got 1.63× fair value from only ₹34 Cr of buys. Punjab Kings spent the most, ₹108 Cr, and got only 0.72×.

Across all 14 seasons, CSK (1.19) and RR (1.14) run the most efficient auctions.

Now the important chart. Each dot is one team in one season. How much a team spent has no link to its win percentage: the correlation is r = −0.03. How well it spent does: Value Index against wins gives r = +0.35.

So what: spending more doesn't win matches; spending well does.

3Player Value Map

Q2 · 1:00
ShowThe scatter. Point to the two dashed median lines and the four corners. Hover one dot to show the player tooltip.

Every dot is one player bought at auction. Price runs left to right; Impact Points run bottom to top. The dashed lines are that season's median price and median impact, which split players into four groups:

Stars: expensive and high impact. Steals: cheap but high impact. Overpaid: expensive but low impact. Passengers: cheap and low impact.

In 2026 there were 24 steals and 8 overpaid buys. (Hover a dot.) Hovering shows the player's card.

So what: keep the Steals and Stars; the Overpaid and Passengers are the release list before the next auction.

4Steals and Overpaid

Q2 · 1:00
ShowThe Top-10 steals and Top-10 overpaid tables, then the Bargain XI and Overpaid XI.

This page names the names. The best steal of 2026 is Kartik Tyagi: bought for just ₹30 lakh, the buy returned 34× fair value. The most overpaid is Rishabh Pant at ₹27 Cr, overpaid by about ₹19.5 Cr.

We rank overpaid players by crores lost, not by the ratio, because the ratio becomes extreme for cheap players.

Below are the Bargain XI, the best-value eleven picked by role, and the Overpaid XI. All five most overpaid players cost ₹14 Cr or more.

So what: bid on expected output, not on reputation.

5Role and Nationality

Q4 · 0:50
ShowAverage price by role (Indian vs overseas), then Value Index by role.

Are overseas players worth their premium? In 2026, overseas buys cost 2.4× as much as Indian buys but returned less per crore: 0.88 against 1.11.

Across all seasons, Indian bowlers are the best-value role at 1.22, and overseas bowlers the worst at 0.84.

So what: use overseas slots only for proven match-winners, and build depth with Indian players.

6Price Brackets

Q6 · 0:50
ShowValue Index by price bracket (it falls from left to right), then the quadrant mix per bracket.

Do cheap buys beat marquee signings? Per crore, clearly yes. In 2026, buys under ₹2 Cr returned 2.7–3.5× fair value, while ₹10 Cr+ signings returned only 0.55×.

Over all seasons, base-price buys give 4.1× and ₹10 Cr+ buys give 0.49×. To be fair to stars: marquee players beat median impact 88% of the time. They do perform, but each crore buys far more output in the cheap brackets.

7Player Profile

Drill-through · 0:30
ShowOn the Value Map or Steals page, right-click a player → Drill through → Player Profile.

From any player, you can right-click and drill through to their profile. It shows career totals, price and Impact Points for every season, the value group each season, and the full auction history. This is how a scout checks a single player before bidding.

8Methodology and data

0:30
ShowThe Methodology page: formulas and sources.

The data comes from the Kaggle IPL Auction dataset for prices and Cricsheet for ball-by-ball data. We cleaned the player names, fixed 96 mismatches by hand, and checked our totals against official auction figures and the Orange and Purple Caps. We built a star-schema data model in Power BI, and all measures are written in DAX.

9Web app with MongoDB

0:55
ShowOpen the web app. Click through two pages quickly, then open the Squad Builder.

We also built the same dashboard as a MERN web app, so anyone can open it in a browser without Power BI. The pages and the numbers are the same as the ones we just showed.

The only difference is where the data lives. We exported the cleaned Power BI model into MongoDB Atlas: 8 collections, about 7,100 documents. An Express API reads from MongoDB, React draws the charts, and it's deployed on Vercel.

The web app adds two things: player search and a Squad Builder that picks the best squad within a salary cap.

Data flow, in one line: raw CSVs → Power BI model (DAX) → exported tables → MongoDB Atlas → Express API → React web app.

10Conclusion

0:40

To conclude, three lessons for any franchise going into an auction:

One: spending more does not win matches; spending efficiently does. Two: the best value is in cheap, under-₹2-Cr buys and in Indian bowlers. Three: be careful with ₹10 Cr+ signings and overseas premiums, and buy output per crore, not names.

Thank you, sir. We're happy to take questions.

Likely questions: short answers

  • Why not just use runs and wickets? They can't be added together. Impact Points put batting, bowling and fielding on one scale, so all-rounders and keepers are compared fairly.
  • Why compare with each season's own market? Prices doubled over time. Using shares of that season's spend and impact removes inflation, so 2013 and 2026 can be compared.
  • What about retained players? They have no auction price, so they are left out of the value measures and marked "Unpriced".
  • Why MongoDB for the web app? The data is read-only tables of players and seasons, which store naturally as JSON documents. The free Atlas tier hosts it in the cloud, and it fits the MERN stack.
  • Limitations? Base prices only exist for 2025–26, 2013 prices are converted at ₹54 per US dollar, and Impact Points don't capture captaincy or match situation.
